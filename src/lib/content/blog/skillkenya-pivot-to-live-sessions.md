Why we're abandoning the traditional course model and embracing real-time, interactive learning sessions that actually work for Kenyan learners.

## What is Skill Kenya?

[Skill Kenya](https://skillkenya.com) is our professional online learning platform, designed to offer high-quality education in **programming, design, marketing, and animation** to Kenyan learners and beyond.

When we launched the platform, we followed the traditional online course model: create pre-recorded video courses, build course modules, set them up for self-paced learning, and let students work through them independently.

The platform covers four areas:

- **Programming** — web development, mobile apps, backend systems
- **Design** — UI/UX, graphic design, brand identity
- **Marketing** — digital marketing, social media, content strategy
- **Animation** — motion graphics, 2D/3D animation, video editing

But here's the problem: **the traditional course model isn't working** — for us or our students.

## Why Pre-Recorded Courses Are Failing

1. **Low completion rates.** Students start courses with enthusiasm but rarely finish. Without live accountability, motivation drops after the first few lessons. The average completion rate for online courses is under 15%.
2. **No real-time support.** When students get stuck, they wait hours or days for an email response. By the time help arrives, they've lost momentum or given up. Learning happens best when questions are answered immediately.
3. **Content creation bottleneck.** Quality pre-recorded courses are extremely time-consuming — each hour of final video takes 5–10 hours of scripting, recording, editing and polishing. We can't scale content creation fast enough to meet demand.
4. **Outdated content.** Tech moves fast. By the time a course is edited and published, frameworks have updated and best practices have evolved.
5. **No community or networking.** Students learn in isolation, missing peer learning, collaboration, and the energy of learning alongside others.
6. **Complex technical setup.** Course platforms, payment systems, video hosting, dashboards, progress tracking — it's complex and expensive. We spend more time maintaining infrastructure than teaching.

> **The core issue.** We built a system optimised for content creation and distribution, not for actual learning outcomes and student success. The method simply doesn't work for the way people actually learn.

## The New Model: Live Interactive Sessions

Instead of fighting the limitations of pre-recorded courses, we're embracing what actually works: **live, scheduled, interactive learning sessions**.

- **Scheduled live classes.** Classes happen at specific times (e.g. "Web Development — every Saturday, 2–5pm"). Students join via Google Meet or Zoom. It's like attending a real class, from anywhere.
- **Real-time interaction.** Students ask questions immediately; instructors see confusion, adjust pacing, share screens for live coding, and give instant feedback. Learning becomes a conversation, not a monologue.
- **Cohort-based learning.** Students learn together in small groups of 15–25. They build relationships, collaborate, hold each other accountable, and form a support network that lasts beyond the classes.
- **Flexible content delivery.** No weeks spent producing perfect videos — instructors prepare slides and demos, teach live, and adapt to student needs in real time.
- **Optional recordings.** Sessions are recorded and shared with enrolled students for review, but the focus is on attending live. Recordings are supplementary, not the primary method.
- **Simpler infrastructure.** No complex course platform — just a simple site for scheduling and payments, plus Google Meet or Zoom. Lower overhead means more focus on teaching quality.

**Example session structure.** A Full-Stack Web Development bootcamp: every Tuesday and Thursday, 6–8pm EAT, over eight weeks — 60 minutes of live teaching, then Q&A, then 40 minutes of live coding, plus a homework assignment. Platform: Google Meet. Cohort size: 20 students maximum.

## The Hybrid Future (If We Manage)

We're not completely abandoning courses — we're being realistic about priorities. Live sessions come first because they deliver better results and are faster to launch.

**If we manage** to build momentum and create systems that work, we can add pre-recorded courses as **supplementary content** alongside the live sessions:

- **Live sessions (primary)** — scheduled weekly classes, real-time teaching, cohort accountability, project-based learning, premium pricing.
- **Courses (supplementary)** — self-paced video lessons, pre-work for live sessions, reference material, a lower-cost entry point, an upsell into live cohorts.

For example: a student might take a self-paced "JavaScript Fundamentals" course to build the basics, then join a live "React Bootcamp" cohort for structured, interactive learning.

## How We'll Implement This

- **Phase 1 — immediate (weeks 1–4).** Simplify the website (remove complex course infrastructure, add a session calendar and booking), choose a platform (Google Meet or Zoom), launch 2–3 beginner pilot classes, set up M-Pesa payments, and market heavily on Instagram, TikTok and WhatsApp.
- **Phase 2 — growth (months 2–3).** Expand offerings based on demand, build community groups for each cohort, hire specialist instructors, archive session recordings for enrolled students, and collect testimonials.
- **Phase 3 — scale (months 4–6).** Run multiple cohorts on different schedules, add advanced multi-week bootcamps, offer corporate training for employee upskilling, and — if bandwidth allows — start creating self-paced courses from recorded sessions.

## Why This Model Will Succeed

- **Higher completion rates.** Live accountability and scheduled classes mean students actually finish — cohort-based learning sees 60–80% completion versus 5–15% for self-paced courses.
- **Better learning outcomes.** Real-time Q&A, personalised feedback and hands-on projects lead to deeper understanding and real skill.
- **Faster to market.** We can launch a new session in days, not months — testing topics and iterating quickly.
- **Community building.** Students build real relationships and a support system that outlasts the course.
- **Premium positioning.** Live instruction justifies higher prices — students pay for expert time and attention, not just content.
- **Always current.** Teaching live means content updates in real time. No outdated videos.

## The Path Forward

We're not giving up on Skill Kenya — we're pivoting to a model that actually serves our students and makes business sense. Pre-recorded courses sound great in theory, but live sessions deliver real results.

The transition will happen strategically:

1. Launch live sessions immediately with simple infrastructure.
2. Focus on student success and completion rates.
3. Build community and referral loops for organic growth.
4. Scale what works before adding complexity.
5. Add courses only if and when we have proven systems in place.

> The best learning happens in conversation, not in isolation. We're choosing real connection over perfect production.
