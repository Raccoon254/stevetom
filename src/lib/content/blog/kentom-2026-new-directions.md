A realistic roadmap for expanding beyond digital services into physical creative production, exploring what AI can't replace: craftsmanship, local relationships, and tangible value.

## Where We Are Today

Currently, Kentom operates as a freelance creative studio, taking on projects through a combination of **Fiverr gigs** and **direct client relationships**. While this model generates revenue, it's far from optimized for maximum earnings or sustainable growth.

The current reality:

- **Platform dependency** — Fiverr takes 20% commission on every project, limiting profit margins.
- **Unoptimized workflow** — ad-hoc project management leads to inefficiencies and missed opportunities.
- **Undocumented projects** — significant work on projects like dishpoa.com (food delivery), Chezapay (payment platform), and qailly.com (quality assurance) remains largely undocumented and undermonetized.
- **No scalable systems** — growth is limited by available hours rather than systems and processes.
- **Service-only revenue** — 100% active income. If we don't work, we don't earn.

> **The problem.** This reactive, platform-dependent model leaves money on the table. We're trading time for dollars with no leverage, no product lines, and no passive income. The expertise exists, but the business structure doesn't maximize its value.

## The Strategic Vision: A New Direction

As we enter 2026, the creative industry faces a fundamental shift. AI tools have democratized digital content creation, making design, copywriting, and basic visual work accessible to everyone. But this shift reveals an opportunity: **the growing value of physical, hands-on creativity that AI cannot replicate**.

Kentom is positioned to pivot toward services that combine digital design expertise with physical production, local market understanding, and authentic human craftsmanship. This isn't about abandoning digital — it's about expanding into spaces where our human touch creates irreplaceable value.

> While AI generates pixels, humans create products. While algorithms suggest designs, craftspeople deliver quality. While bots post content, people build relationships.

## Path 1: Custom Apparel & Print Production

Every business, school, church, and sports team in Kenya needs branded apparel. AI can suggest designs, but it can't operate a heat press, ensure colour accuracy, or hand-deliver quality products to local customers.

**Revenue model**

- Custom t-shirts — KES 600–1,200 per piece (40–50% margin)
- Bulk orders — KES 400–700 per piece (schools, events, companies)
- Premium work — KES 1,000–2,000 (embroidery, specialty prints)

**First-quarter target:** 40–60 pieces a month, KES 35,000–55,000 revenue — conservative, assuming part-time operation while building a client base.

**Investment required:** a quality entry-level heat press (KES 40,000–55,000), initial materials (KES 15,000–25,000), and sample inventory (KES 10,000–15,000) — roughly **KES 65,000–95,000** total. Break-even is expected in 2–3 months with consistent orders.

## Path 2: 3D Printing Services

Kenya's innovation ecosystem is growing, but affordable local 3D printing services remain scarce. From architects needing models to inventors prototyping ideas, the demand exists — it just needs to be tapped.

**Service offerings**

- Prototypes — KES 1,500–8,000 per project
- Custom phone cases — KES 400–800 each
- Architectural models — KES 5,000–25,000
- Replacement parts — KES 300–3,000
- Custom gifts & decor — KES 250–1,500

**First-quarter target:** 12–20 projects a month, KES 25,000–45,000 revenue. **Investment:** an entry 3D printer (KES 38,000–48,000), filament, tools and supplies — roughly **KES 47,000–63,000** total.

Marketing doubles as education: a "3D Print Kenya" channel with tutorials, showcases and time-lapses, plus partnerships with innovation hubs, architects and inventors.

## Path 3: Full-Service Digital Marketing

While AI can create content, it cannot understand local market nuances, build client relationships, or execute strategic campaigns. Small Kenyan businesses need more than templates — they need partners who understand their customers.

**Service packages**

- **Starter — KES 18,000–28,000.** Logo and brand colours, social media setup, basic landing page, a two-week content calendar, Google Business setup.
- **Growth — KES 35,000–55,000.** Everything in Starter, plus a professional website, a month of content and posting, basic SEO, email marketing, and a product photography session.
- **Premium — KES 75,000–120,000.** Everything in Growth, plus custom website development, three months of managed social media, two professional videos, paid ads management, and monthly analytics and strategy calls.

**First-quarter target:** 3–5 quality clients, KES 70,000–140,000 monthly revenue. The setup cost is the lowest of the four paths (KES 16,000–30,000) and leverages existing skills and hardware, so it can start immediately.

## Path 4: Video Production & Content Creation

Every business needs video content, but quality local production is expensive. By starting with smartphone setups and scaling gradually, we can offer affordable options while building a portfolio and YouTube presence.

**Pricing**

- Social media reels/shorts — KES 3,000–8,000 per video
- Product videos — KES 8,000–25,000
- Corporate videos — KES 20,000–60,000
- Event coverage — KES 15,000–45,000
- Testimonial videos — KES 5,000–15,000

**Phased equipment.** Phase 1 is a smartphone setup — gimbal, mic, ring light, tripod, free editing software — for KES 18,000–30,000. Phase 2, a semi-pro mirrorless setup at KES 90,000–150,000, comes only after 4–6 months once revenue justifies it.

**First-quarter target:** 3–5 videos a month, KES 20,000–45,000 revenue, starting with reels and testimonials while building a portfolio.

## Realistic Financial Projections

**Quarter 1 (Jan–Mar 2026).** Total investment needed: KES 80,000–150,000 (choose 1–2 primary service lines to start). Expected monthly revenue by the end of Q1: KES 85,000–165,000, combining two service lines.

**Quarter 2–3 growth.** Monthly revenue rising to KES 140,000–250,000 in Q2 and KES 200,000–350,000 in Q3, with operating costs at 40–50%.

**Year 1 (conservative).** Total annual revenue of KES 1.8M–3.2M across all service lines, with net profit of KES 900K–1.9M after reinvestment and expenses.

Key assumptions: start from home with no rent overhead, handle most work yourself initially, hire freelancers only as needed, market through organic social media and word of mouth, buy equipment gradually from profits, and reinvest 30–40% of profit for growth.

## Recommended Starting Path

**Digital marketing + one physical service.** Start with digital marketing (immediate cash flow, low investment), then add either apparel printing or video production based on your interest and local demand.

- **Option A — Marketing + Apparel.** Best if you prefer tangible products and have space for equipment. Investment KES 80K–120K, Q1 target KES 85K–135K/month, break-even 2–3 months.
- **Option B — Marketing + Video.** Best if you enjoy storytelling and want a YouTube presence. Investment KES 35K–65K, Q1 target KES 75K–125K/month, break-even 1–2 months.

Why this works: digital marketing generates immediate cash flow, the physical service differentiates you from pure digital agencies, there's a natural cross-sell (branding plus branded merchandise), your own marketing showcases your services, and starting from home keeps the risk low.

## Implementation Timeline

- **Week 1–2 — Foundation.** Business registration and bank account, domain and email, social handles, basic branding, equipment research.
- **Week 3–4 — Launch preparation.** Website launch, purchase of primary equipment, sample products or demo videos, 20–30 prepared social posts.
- **Month 2 — First clients.** A launch promotion, the first 5–10 orders and testimonials, consistent posting, and active outreach to 50+ potential clients.
- **Month 3 — Scale & refine.** 20–30 completed projects, a YouTube channel or tutorial content, a Q1 performance review, and a possible third service line.

## Keys to Success

- **Quality over quantity** — perfect 2–3 services before expanding; every project should be portfolio-worthy.
- **Document everything** — before/after photos and video testimonials; every project is marketing for the next.
- **Build relationships** — a personal touch with every client, follow-ups after delivery, and incentivised referrals.
- **Financial discipline** — track every expense, require a 50% deposit upfront, build a three-month emergency fund.
- **Learn continuously** — free tutorials, creator communities, new techniques, staying ahead of local competition.
- **Start lean, scale smart** — work from home initially, hire freelancers before staff, let revenue dictate the pace.

## The Path Forward

These projections aren't dreams — they're achievable targets based on conservative estimates and realistic market conditions. The key is starting **now**, starting **small**, and scaling based on actual results.

While AI reshapes digital content creation, physical craftsmanship, local service delivery and authentic human relationships become more valuable. Kentom's opportunity lies at that intersection: **digital expertise meeting physical reality**.
